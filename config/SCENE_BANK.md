# Clarity Lab — Scene Bank

This file is appended to every content prompt. Edit it by hand; no code changes needed.

A scene is one person, one moment, one concrete detail. It is the opening of every Clarity Lab text.

---

## Rules for using a scene

Pick one, or write a new one in the same register.

**Do not reuse a scene within 40 published pieces.** Check the recent-scenes list passed with the topic.

**Change the surface, keep the structure.** The same scene can be a document, an email, a voice note, a call. What must stay is the shape: something small and specific that reveals a gap between what the person knows and what she does.

**Describe the action, never the feeling.** Write *she closed the laptop*, not *she felt overwhelmed*. The feeling belongs to the reader.

**No names.** She, he, or the person. Never invent a name — it turns observation into fiction.

**No professions in the scene itself** unless the piece is specifically about work. The reader should not have to match her job title to enter.

## The bank

**S01 — The open document.** Eleven at night, the document has been open for an hour, nothing written. She knows what it needs to say.

**S02 — The client mirror.** She walks a client through exactly the thing she is stuck in herself, and she does it well.

**S03 — The cancelled session.** Third one cancelled in a row, because she already knows what she will say when she gets there.

**S04 — The convincing explanation.** She explains her situation to a friend so precisely that the friend agrees with her. Afterwards it is worse.

**S05 — The re-decided decision.** Decided on Monday. Undone Wednesday. Decided again Friday, the same way.

**S06 — The fourth course.** Fourth course on the same subject, and less certainty than before the first.

**S07 — The unsent message.** Rewritten four times. Still in drafts.

**S08 — The named pattern.** She can name the pattern in one word. It has not moved since she learned the word.

**S09 — The seventh month of thinking.** She said she needed to think about it. That was in January.

**S10 — Three reasonable options.** All three make sense. None has been chosen. The list has not changed in weeks.

**S11 — The smooth answer.** Someone asks how she is doing with it and the answer comes out so complete that they do not ask a second question.

**S12 — The unsent voice note.** Eleven minutes recorded. Deleted without sending.

**S13 — The rescheduled morning.** She moved the block for it to next week. It has been next week since June.

**S14 — The research tab.** Nineteen tabs open on a question she answered months ago.

**S15 — The advice she gives.** She tells someone else exactly what to do, in one sentence, without hesitating.

**S16 — The reread notebook.** She opens the notes from a year ago and finds the same sentence, written in the same words.

## Adding a new scene

Format: **Sxx — short label.** One or two sentences, present tense, action only.

Test it against three questions before adding:

1. Could this describe almost anyone? Then it is too wide. Cut it.
2. Does it name a feeling? Rewrite it as an action.
3. Does the reader have to be told what it means? Then it is not a scene, it is an argument.
