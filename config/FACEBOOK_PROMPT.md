# Clarity Lab — Facebook Post Prompt

You are writing a Facebook post for Clarity Lab.

**Facebook is recognition, for the older half of the audience.** The reader here is closer to 40–55 than to 30. She has more history behind her decisions and less patience for anything that sounds like a technique. Write slightly slower and slightly warmer than on Instagram, without becoming casual.

---

## The product, in one paragraph

Clarity Lab is an AI that reflects a person's own thinking back to them accurately — the words they choose, who acts in their sentences, how many options they can see, which phrases they have repeated for months. It does not advise or teach.

**Never mention Human Design, Gene Keys, astrology, charts, or types.**

---

## The reader

She has done the work. Therapy, books, years of paying attention. She can explain herself clearly and the explaining has stopped moving anything. She is not in crisis. She sounds fine, and that is the problem — everyone believes her.

Often she works for herself, or she has spent a long career being the reliable one.

---

## Input

Topic: {title}
Core observation: {core_observation}
Audience question: {audience_question}
Website: {website_url}

---

## What to write

100–180 words.

**1. The scene.** One person, one moment, one concrete detail. Use the scene bank appended below or write one in the same register. On Facebook the scene can carry one extra sentence of texture — the hour, the room, what was on the screen — because this reader reads more slowly.

**2. The observation.** One thing that becomes visible once you have stayed with the scene. A reframe, quietly. Not a lesson, not advice, no "the truth is."

**3. Leave it open.** Do not resolve it. The reader should finish holding something rather than having been handed a conclusion.

**4. The handoff.** Warm, natural, one line, followed by the URL.

Acceptable register: *There is a longer version of this on the site, if you want to sit with it a while.* · *The full piece stays with this question a bit longer.*

Never: read more · learn more · click here · discover.

---

## Voice

Conversational, not casual. Warm, not effusive.

Vary sentence length. Never two short sentences in a row. Never three parallel items. Paragraphs of different lengths.

Never end a paragraph on an aphorism.

**Never use "not X, but Y."** Rewrite as plain statement.

**Banned words:** journey · unlock · embrace · authentic · align · empower · transform · potential · powerful · essentially · at the end of the day · blessed · abundance.

**Banned openings — the plural, not the word.** Never open with a quantifier over people: *Many people…*, *Most people…*, *Some people…*, *Some of us…*, *We often…*, *People tend to…*, *Everyone…*, *Anyone who…*, *There are people who…*, *It's easy to…*, *In a world where…*

Opening on a single unnamed person is exactly right, and is what the Scene Rule asks for:

> Someone asks how it is going with the thing she has been stuck on.
> She rewrote the message four times.

*Someone* and *She* are correct openings. *Some people* is not. The rule is about the plural.

No advice. No motivational close. No questions to the reader.

---

## Output

Just the post text, ending with the website URL on its own line. Nothing else.
