# Clarity Lab — Image Generation Prompt

## Master Image Rules

Create a still photograph for a reflective Clarity Lab article titled "{title}".

The Clarity Lab visual world should feel **observed rather than designed**.

These are fragments of real life: places someone has just left, places someone is about to enter, ordinary objects carrying traces of use, streets, rooms, weather, movement, distance, imperfect domestic spaces, transitional spaces, and occasional anonymous human presence.

The feed should feel like a collection of moments from one thoughtful person's life across different days and places.

It should **not** look like:

* a stone or ceramics brand
* an interiors catalogue
* a wellness account
* a meditation app
* a luxury lifestyle campaign
* a collection of abstract textures
* a stock-photo library

Subject family: {subject_state}.
Composition: {composition_state}.
Light: {light_state}.
Palette: {visual_state_palette}.
Mood: {visual_state_mood}.
Optional accent: {accent_state}.
Journey state name, for reference only: {visual_state_name}.

Context for tone only: {core_observation}

---

## Relationship to the article

**Do not literally illustrate the article.**

If the article is about uncertainty, do not automatically generate a crossroads.

If it is about choices, do not generate two doors.

If it is about feeling different, do not generate one object separated from a group.

If it is about change, do not generate a road disappearing into the distance.

The photograph belongs emotionally beside the article without turning the article into a visual metaphor.

The connection may come through atmosphere, human trace, scale, interruption, distance, light, or an ordinary detail.

---

## Human presence

People are allowed occasionally.

They should never become the subject in the manner of a portrait or lifestyle advertisement.

Allowed:

* a person far away in a large space
* someone seen from behind
* a figure crossing a street
* someone sitting alone at the far end of a room
* two people small within a larger environment
* a partially visible person entering or leaving the frame
* a blurred figure created naturally by movement
* a coat, bag, chair, glass, notebook, or other trace suggesting someone was recently there

Avoid:

* faces as the focal point
* posed portraits
* smiling stock-photo people
* dramatic emotional expressions
* hands posed around objects
* staged "woman thinking by window" imagery
* a lonely figure obviously used as a metaphor
* fashion-editorial posing

Human presence should make the world feel inhabited, not explain the article.

---

## No text

No readable text.

No letters as a focal element.

No numbers as a focal element.

No logos.

No brand marks.

No readable signage.

No readable screens.

If environmental text naturally exists far in the background, it must be indistinct and unreadable.

---

## Photographic register

Photographic, natural, contemporary.

Realistic light.

Real surfaces.

Real imperfection.

Natural depth.

The photograph may be beautiful, but it should not look beautified for sale.

Allow:

* grain
* imperfect weather
* ordinary architecture
* slightly awkward framing
* objects partly outside the frame
* uneven surfaces
* traces of use
* mixed natural materials
* lived-in spaces
* atmospheric distance
* imperfect light

Avoid:

* CGI
* 3D rendering
* illustration
* painterly effects
* fantasy lighting
* HDR
* excessive blur
* artificial cinematic drama
* luxury-product photography
* pristine showroom styling

Square format.

---

## Do not produce

These are recurring visual clichés and should not be generated.

* Zen stones stacked or balanced
* isolated rocks photographed like products
* marble or mineral samples as the main subject
* ceramic objects arranged as still life
* a single lit candle
* a minimal white desk with laptop and plant
* coffee cup by a window
* open book on a bed or blanket
* mirror used as metaphor
* door standing dramatically ajar
* crossroads
* empty road disappearing toward horizon
* bridge used as transition metaphor
* staircase used as progress metaphor
* single feather
* dandelion
* butterfly
* raindrops on glass in soft focus
* generic beach sunset
* mountains at sunrise
* perfectly arranged flat lay
* chess pieces
* compass
* clock
* lightbulb
* key or keyhole
* puzzle pieces
* maze
* lone chair in an empty white room
* one object deliberately separated from identical objects
* perfectly centred symmetrical composition
* generic woman staring thoughtfully through a window
* hands holding a mug, notebook, phone, or face
* decorative piles of stones, paper, linen, glass, or wood

# Visual Journey

The palette changes gradually across thirteen states.

The movement should feel continuous rather than like thirteen separate filters.

Colours describe the atmosphere of the photograph. They should never tint every object artificially.

0 | Morning Mist

# Subject Families

Subject families control **what kind of world we enter**, rather than rotating through decorative materials.

Advance one step per published article and wrap after 9.

## 0 | Lived-in interior

An ordinary room carrying evidence of recent human activity.

Examples:

* chair pulled slightly away from a table
* jacket left over the back of a chair
* half-open curtain
* shoes near an entrance
* blanket folded imperfectly on a sofa
* glass left on a table
* an unmade corner of a room

Never style the objects into a still life.

---

## 1 | Street and neighbourhood

A real street observed during an ordinary moment.

Examples:

* quiet intersection
* laundromat exterior
* apartment entrance
* bus stop
* corner store
* parked bicycles
* residential sidewalk
* crosswalk after rain
* small-town main street
* anonymous city block

Avoid famous landmarks and travel photography.

---

## 2 | Transitional space

Places designed for being between one place and another.

Examples:

* airport waiting area
* station platform
* hotel corridor
* elevator lobby
* parking garage
* building entrance
* ferry terminal
* waiting room
* hallway
* escalator landing

No symbolic "journey" treatment. It is simply a real place.

---

## 3 | Table after use

Evidence of something that already happened rather than an arranged still life.

Examples:

* two glasses after a conversation
* papers shifted across a working table
* one chair pushed back
* plate after breakfast
* open notebook with writing completely unreadable
* charging cable
* receipts turned face-down
* crumbs
* water ring

Nothing arranged neatly for the camera.

---

## 4 | Weather and exterior

Weather interacting with ordinary surroundings.

Examples:

* wet pavement
* wind moving a curtain
* snow beside parked cars
* late afternoon shadow on a building
* fog between ordinary houses
* sun hitting an apartment wall
* puddle reflecting only light and architecture
* trees moving before rain

Avoid postcard landscapes.

---

## 5 | Anonymous human presence

A person or several people exist naturally inside a larger environment.

Examples:

* one figure crossing a wide street
* someone sitting far away in a waiting area
* person seen from behind entering a building
* two people at opposite ends of a room
* blurred commuter passing through the edge of frame
* figure small against ordinary architecture

The environment remains at least as important as the person.

No portrait.

---

## 6 | Work in progress

Real evidence of thinking, making, fixing, organising, or working.

Examples:

* desk midway through actual work
* cables and equipment
* papers moved around during a task
* open laptop seen from too far away to read
* tools after use
* packaging partly opened
* whiteboard photographed too obliquely to read
* chair temporarily moved
* unfinished practical task

Never create the polished "productive desk" stock-photo look.

---

## 7 | Domestic threshold

The small boundaries between private spaces.

Examples:

* entryway
* coat hooks
* kitchen doorway
* edge of a bedroom seen from a hall
* apartment landing
* laundry area
* balcony door
* shoes crossing from tile to wood
* light from one room entering another

Do not turn the threshold into a metaphor.

---

## 8 | Distance and scale

A scene in which space is more important than the object.

Examples:

* large cloudy sky above ordinary buildings
* distant person in a public square
* broad field beside a road
* empty section of parking lot
* distant apartment windows
* long wall with one small interruption
* horizon obscured by weather
* large interior with activity far away

Avoid epic landscapes and inspirational imagery.

---

# Composition

Advance one step per published article and wrap after 7.

## 0 | Extreme close

A real fragment fills much of the frame.

Scale may initially be unclear, but the image must still feel connected to a real place rather than abstract texture photography.

## 1 | Off-centre observation

The subject sits noticeably away from the centre.

The empty space feels accidental and natural.

## 2 | Environmental wide

The surrounding space dominates.

People or objects occupy only a small portion of the frame.

## 3 | High viewpoint

Looking down naturally from standing height, a balcony, stairs, or another plausible position.

Never a styled flat lay.

## 4 | Looking through

A foreground edge partly obscures the scene.

Examples: doorway edge, curtain, seat back, architectural column.

The effect should feel observational, not voyeuristic.

## 5 | Edge of frame

The most important visual information is partly cropped or placed close to one edge.

The photograph feels caught rather than composed.

## 6 | Unequal division

Architecture, shadow, horizon, furniture, or another real line divides the frame into unequal areas.

Avoid perfect symmetry.

---

# Light

Advance one step per published article and wrap after 5.

## 0 | Low directional daylight

Morning or late-afternoon light entering at an angle.

Natural contrast and long imperfect shadows.

## 1 | Overcast daylight

Soft, even, realistic light.

Muted colour without becoming grey or lifeless.

## 2 | Window falloff

Natural daylight strongest near a real window or opening and gradually disappearing into the room.

## 3 | Dusk and practical light

Remaining daylight mixed with ordinary lamps, windows, streetlights, or building light.

Never cinematic neon.

## 4 | Reflected bright daylight

Light bouncing naturally from another building, pale wall, pavement, snow, or unseen exterior surface.

Bright without looking artificially lit.

---

# Accent States

The accent is optional.

It should usually appear as something naturally present in the environment rather than a colour deliberately placed for composition.

Advance one step per published article and wrap after 6.

0 | muted terracotta
1 | soft amber
2 | dusty coral
3 | muted lilac
4 | deep olive
5 | ocean teal

Possible natural sources include clothing far in frame, upholstery, painted architecture, an ordinary object, evening light, vegetation, or weather.

Never insert an object solely because its colour matches the accent.

---

# How the rotation works

Each list advances independently with every published article:

```text
palette     = journey[i % 13]
subject     = subjects[i % 9]
composition = compositions[i % 7]
light       = lights[i % 5]
accent      = accents[i % 6]
```

The independent cycles prevent consecutive images from repeatedly combining the same subject, framing, light, and palette.

The mathematical rotation is only a diversity mechanism.

**It must never become visible as a formula in the feed.**

If two generated images still look substantially alike despite having different states, visual similarity matters more than the state labels. Regenerate the newer image.

---

# Feed-level diversity rule

Before accepting an image, compare it visually with recent Clarity Lab images.

Reject and regenerate when the new image repeats the dominant visual idea of a recent image, including:

* another close-up of a material
* another empty architectural corner
* another lone object against a wall
* another distant figure in nearly identical framing
* another table photographed from the same angle
* another nearly identical blue-grey scene
* another image whose main impression is "quiet beige object"

Different rotation metadata does **not** make two visually similar images different.

The feed should alternate naturally between:

* inside and outside
* near and far
* inhabited and temporarily empty
* warm and cool
* object-scale and environment-scale
* structured architecture and irregular everyday life

Do not force a perfect sequence. Avoid visible sameness.

# Editing this file

This file is the human-editable visual direction for Clarity Lab.

The palette, subject families, composition, light, and accent lists are read from this file at run time.

They may be rewritten by hand without changing Python code.

Keep the list counts:

* 13 palette states
* 9 subject families
* 7 composition states
* 5 light states
* 6 accent states

Changing the wording inside a state is safe.

If a future strategy requires changing the number of states, treat that as a configuration-design change rather than silently editing the arithmetic.

The goal of this file is not to produce thousands of mathematically unique combinations.

The goal is for someone scrolling the Clarity Lab feed to feel that **the same mind is looking at many different parts of life — rather than photographing the same collection of stones in different colours.**
